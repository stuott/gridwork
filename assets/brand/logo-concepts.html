Gridwork

Six marks for the variant-sudoku workbench. The name does two jobs at once — the grid you solve on, and -work the way latticework and brickwork use it: something assembled by hand, one deliberate piece at a time. Each mark below leans on one half or the other.

Must survive
16 px favicon and taskbar icon. If it fills in at that size, it's out.
One colour
Drawn on currentColor, matching the toolbar icon set.
Six themes
Cool, warm and nebula × light and dark. No per-theme variants.
Not a puzzle
The app never solves. The mark shouldn't look like a finished grid.

The marks

MARK 01

Latticework

Four bars woven over and under, checkerboard fashion. Read fast it's a grid; read slowly it's something made — which is the half of the name that most sudoku branding never touches.

It also sidesteps the obvious problem with a 9-cell grid mark: every sudoku product already has one.

Trade-off: the weave gaps close up below about 20 px. At favicon size it degrades to a clean hash, which is acceptable but generic. Thickening the bars to 4.4 buys back some of it.

MARK 02

Working Cell

A 3×3 box, empty except for three pencil marks in the middle cell. It says the thing the whole app is built around: candidates narrowed, digit not placed. Nobody has finished this puzzle, and nobody is going to finish it for you.

The dots are the only accent-coloured element in the set, which makes this the most theme-reactive mark — it visibly changes character across the six palettes.

MARK 03

Nine Points

No rules at all — nine positions, three resolved. It's a grid stated entirely by where things sit, which is exactly how a solver reads a board: the lines are scaffolding, the information is in the cells.

The three filled points are rotationally placed rather than in a row, so it reads as work in progress rather than a solved line.

MARK 04

Centre Box

Only the box dividers — the two heavy lines each way that turn nine cells into a sudoku — with the centre box filled. The heavy rule is the one piece of grid geometry that belongs to sudoku and not to graph paper.

Confident and blunt at any size, and it borrows nothing from the toolbar's grid icon, so the two can coexist.

MARK 05

The G Grid

A squared-off G whose strokes run along grid lines, with the rest of the lattice showing faintly behind. The letterform isn't drawn on the grid — it's cut from it.

This is the only mark that carries the name without the wordmark, which matters for a taskbar icon or a desktop shortcut where no type appears.

MARK 06

On the Table

The grid seen in perspective, the way it sits on a desk in front of you rather than flat on a screen. Warmest of the six, and the only one that suggests a person leaning over the puzzle.

Pairs naturally with the warm theme; the accent cell reads as the one you're currently in.

Wordmark

Gridwork

Archivo SemiBold, −4.5% tracking. A grotesque with squared terminals and tight counters — engineered rather than friendly. The tight tracking packs the letters like cells in a row.

gridwork

IBM Plex Mono Medium, lowercase. Monospace puts every letter on its own fixed cell, which is the joke and the point. Reads more like a tool than a product — appropriate for something you run locally.

gridwork

Weight split. Two words, two weights, no space — makes the compound legible at a glance and gives you a natural short form (work) for tabs and window titles.

Where I'd go. 01 Latticework is the most distinctive and the best fit for the name; 03 Nine Points is the safest engineering choice because it survives every size you'll actually ship at. A common resolution is to run 01 as the brand mark and 03 as the favicon — they share a 3×3 skeleton, so they don't read as two logos.

All six are drawn on a 32-unit box with stroke="currentColor", the same contract ui/icons.ts uses, so any of them drops into the icon set without a per-theme variant.
